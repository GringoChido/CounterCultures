## Prompt

Create a pitch conversation between two hosts presenting the Counter Cultures digital transformation project to Roger, the owner of Counter Cultures — a premium bath and kitchen fixtures showroom in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.

**Tone:** Conversational, warm, confident. Like two people who genuinely love this project walking Roger through what they've built for him. Not salesy, not corporate — more like friends who happen to be really good at what they do. Roger is not technical — use plain language. Make him feel understood. Let the excitement come through naturally.

**Pacing:** Don't rush. Let the story breathe. Spend time painting pictures — the showroom, San Miguel, the artisan workshops, the copper basin catching light in a colonial courtyard. The best pitches feel like stories, not presentations. When you describe something, let the listener see it.

**THE CENTRAL THEME — DRIVE THIS HOME THROUGHOUT:**
This pitch is NOT about fixing a store. It's NOT about making a prettier website. It is about building a **connected system** — a complete digital infrastructure custom-built for Counter Cultures, where the brand, the website, the sales dashboard, the marketing engine, and the team tools all talk to each other.

This is not an off-the-shelf solution. We are not plugging Roger into someone else's software. We are building his system — from scratch — designed around how Counter Cultures actually works. The website, the dashboard, the automations — all of it is custom-built and owned by Roger. That's a fundamentally different thing than buying a subscription to a platform.

Every section of this pitch should reinforce the connected system idea. The showroom already works. What we're building is everything around it — the invisible system that captures demand, routes it to the right people, follows up automatically, and gives Roger visibility into his entire operation.

Use the phrase "connected system" multiple times. Make it clear that each deliverable (brand, site, dashboard, content, SEO, enablement) is not a separate project — they are parts of one integrated system where data flows between them.

**Structure the conversation like this:**

### 1. The Hook — What's Already Working

Take your time here. Set the scene.

Start with San Miguel de Allende itself — the cobblestone streets, the design-forward expat community, the colonial architecture meeting contemporary interiors. This is one of Mexico's most creative cities, and Counter Cultures has been part of that story for 20 years.

Talk about what Roger has built. The showroom experience — walking in and seeing a Kohler freestanding tub next to a hand-hammered copper basin made by an artisan 30 minutes away. That contrast is the whole brand. World-class international fixtures (Kohler, California Faucets, TOTO, Brizo, BLANCO) sitting alongside handcrafted Mexican pieces — copper basins, carved stone sinks, custom tile work. Nobody else in Mexico does this.

The name is great — "Counter Cultures" works in English and Spanish, plays on countertops and counterculture. It signals what they sell AND how they think. 20 years of trust with architects, designers, and homeowners in this market.

Acknowledge all of this genuinely. Then pivot: **"The showroom is not the problem. The problem is that there's no system connecting everything that happens outside the showroom."** The website doesn't feed a dashboard. The dashboard doesn't exist. Leads come in from five different places and nobody can see the full picture. What we're building is the connective tissue — the digital layer that ties everything together.

### 2. The Problem — It's About the Leads

Slow down here. This is the part Roger will feel in his gut because he's lived it.

The real issue is not the website. Roger has told us his team is not responding to leads — they're getting lost. Paint the scenario: A WhatsApp message comes in on a Tuesday afternoon about a freestanding bathtub for a villa renovation. The rep is with a client in the showroom. The message sits there. By Thursday, that customer has called a competitor in Querétaro. A quote gets sent Monday for a $180,000 peso kitchen package — nobody follows up Thursday. By the following week, the architect has moved on.

There's no system to capture, assign, or track leads. There's no visibility — Roger can't answer "how many leads came in last week?" with any confidence. He knows business is happening, but he can't see it. He can't measure it. And the leads he can't see are the ones that disappear.

Emphasize: **this is a systems problem, not a people problem.** His team isn't ignoring leads on purpose. There's just no central place to manage them. No shared view. No follow-up triggers. No accountability built into the workflow. We're building that.

### 3. The Brand Refresh — The Visual Story of Counter Cultures

Walk through the brand refinement with feeling. This isn't just colors and fonts — this is how Counter Cultures will feel to everyone who encounters it.

The name stays. We're not starting over — we're elevating. Everything that follows is designed to reflect what Counter Cultures already is: a place where old-world Mexican craft meets world-class modern design.

**The Color Palette — Drawn from San Miguel Itself:**

Describe this like you're walking through the town and the showroom. Every color in this palette comes from something real — the materials they sell, the architecture of San Miguel, the landscape of the Bajío.

- **Deep Charcoal (#1A1A1A)** — The anchor. Think wrought iron gates on colonial facades, matte black hardware finishes, the weight that grounds everything. Headers, footers, the structural bones of the brand.
- **Terracotta (#C4725A)** — The warmth of San Miguel's rooftops, the clay of artisan workshops, the earth tones that run through every street in town. This replaces a heavy copper accent with something more organic, more Mexican. It's the color of hands shaping clay. Use it for CTAs and moments that need warmth.
- **Aged Copper (#B87333)** — Still here, but as a supporting accent rather than the lead. Literally the color of the copper basins they sell. Used sparingly for the artisanal story — details, highlights, hover states.
- **Desert Sage (#7A8B6F)** — The green of courtyard gardens, prickly pear cactus, herb planters on terraces. A natural, muted green that feels organic and grounded. Use for secondary CTAs, success states, and moments that need life without shouting.
- **Stone Gray (#A89F91)** — Travertine, cantera stone, the marble in their collection. Secondary text, borders, dividers. The quiet texture underneath everything.
- **Warm Linen (#F5F0EB)** — Not white. The color of raw cotton, handwoven textiles, sun-bleached plaster walls. Backgrounds, cards, soft sections. Elegant and warm, never sterile.
- **Sand (#D4C5A9)** — Optional. The color of the desert, of raw materials before they're shaped. Cards, section backgrounds, a second soft tone when Warm Linen needs a companion.

The key insight to call out: **this palette doesn't just look good — it tells the Counter Cultures story.** Every color is traceable to something real. The terracotta rooftops of San Miguel. The copper basins in the showroom. The cantera stone on the facades. The sage green of a courtyard garden. The brand palette IS the world Counter Cultures lives in. That's not decoration — it's identity.

**Typography — The Craft Meets Precision:**
- **Headlines: Refined Serif** (like Canela or Cormorant Garamond) — heritage, craft, permanence. The exact qualities of the brand story. This is what Waterworks and RH do in the luxury space. When you see a serif headline over an image of a copper basin, it immediately says "this is curated, not mass-produced."
- **Body Text: Clean Sans-Serif** (like Inter or DM Sans) — modern, readable, handles English and Spanish well. Doesn't compete with the headlines for attention.
- **Specs/Utility: Monospaced** — for product specifications and pricing. Creates a "design specification" feel that speaks directly to the architect audience.

The type system alone transforms the digital presence. Moving from default system fonts to an intentional serif/sans pairing instantly signals a curated brand experience.

**Photography Direction:** Three sources, no photoshoots needed. $300 Artlist budget for atmospheric San Miguel interiors and lifestyle imagery. Free high-res product photography from Kohler, TOTO, Brizo, BLANCO, and California Faucets dealer portals. Plus Roger's existing showroom and artisan process photos from his camera roll — even iPhone shots, properly color-graded and cropped, become powerful on a well-designed site.

**Wordmark:** Two options to present. Option A refines the existing clean modern direction — a custom-spaced geometric sans-serif with intentionality. Option B leans into the craft story with a refined serif or slab — think how it would look etched into a copper basin or stamped into stone. We present both, and Roger decides where the brand wants to live.

### 4. The Website — A Tour of the New Digital Showroom

Before diving into pages, set the frame: **"The website is not the product. The website is one piece of the connected system."** The site captures leads and feeds them to the dashboard. The blog feeds the SEO engine. The trade portal feeds the CRM. Product pages feed the quote system. Nothing exists in isolation — everything is wired together.

Explain that we're replacing the current Squarespace site with a completely custom-built website — not migrating to another platform, but building something designed specifically for Counter Cultures. The current Squarespace site can't handle trade accounts, has no real product filtering, no lead capture system, and no connection to a dashboard or CRM. We're not patching those limitations — we're starting fresh with a system built around how Roger's business actually works.

Walk through the site map like you're giving Roger a tour of his new building. Make it feel like a narrative, not a checklist:

**"Imagine an architect in Mexico City hears about Counter Cultures from a colleague. She goes to the website..."**

She lands on the **Homepage** — a full-bleed hero image of an artisanal copper basin in a sun-filled San Miguel interior. Below that, the brand bar (Kohler, TOTO, Brizo, BLANCO, California Faucets) signals this is a serious operation. She scrolls past the founder story, sees the shop-by-room navigation, and spots the trade program teaser: "Architects. Designers. Contractors. Welcome."

She clicks through to the **Trade Program portal** — a page that doesn't exist today, and it's the single biggest revenue opportunity. Trade pricing, a dedicated account manager, project specification support, and a simple application form. This is the B2B conversion path that's completely missing right now.

Meanwhile, a homeowner renovating a casa in San Miguel lands on the **Shop pages** — a filterable product grid where she can sort by brand, finish, price, and style. Product cards show the image, brand, name, price, and finish swatches. This filtering is the single biggest UX upgrade over the current site. She clicks into a **Product Detail page** with 5+ images, a finish selector, specs, and two CTAs — "Add to Cart" for smaller items and "Request a Quote" for the $80,000 peso bathtub. Plus a WhatsApp link for questions, because that's how business gets done in Mexico.

Walk through the rest naturally: the **Artisanal Collection** — a dedicated landing page for the handcrafted Mexican pieces with artisan profiles, process stories, and a "Commission a Custom Piece" form (this is the competitive moat — nobody can copy this). **Brand Pages** for each partner brand — Kohler, TOTO, Brizo — each one an SEO play for "[brand] Mexico" searches. The **Project Gallery** where completed installations get credited to the architect or designer (bridges B2C inspiration and B2B recognition). **Our Story** in a magazine-profile format. The **Blog** running three content tracks: Design Inspiration, Product Guides, and Trade Insights — each post ending with a contextual CTA. This blog isn't decoration — it's the SEO and AEO engine.

### 5. The Dashboard — Mission Control

This is the heart of the connected system. Spend real time here — this is what Roger has been asking for without knowing what to call it.

The dashboard is not a separate tool you log into. It's where everything flows. A lead from the website? It shows up here. A WhatsApp message? Here. A walk-in that the team logs? Here. A quote that was sent three days ago and hasn't been followed up? Flagged here, with the rep's name on it.

Paint the picture vividly: **"Roger, imagine this. You open one screen with your morning coffee and you see: 12 new leads this week. 8 have been contacted. 4 need follow-up today — here are their names. Maria closed 3 deals worth $145,000 pesos. Two quotes are sitting unanswered past the 48-hour mark — they're flagged in red. One architect submitted a trade application yesterday and is waiting for approval. You didn't have to call anyone. You didn't have to ask anyone for a report. The system surfaces it."**

This is custom-built — not a Salesforce subscription or a HubSpot login. It's built on Google Sheets as the data backbone (Roger's team already uses Google) with a clean, custom dashboard interface on top. Simple, fast, and designed for how Counter Cultures actually works. Roger owns it. No monthly platform fees. No vendor lock-in.

Cover: shared WhatsApp inbox so multiple reps see and respond to messages, automatic lead assignment based on rules Roger sets, escalation alerts when follow-ups are overdue, and real sales numbers visible in one place.

Then connect it back: **"The website captures the lead. The dashboard tracks it. The automation follows up on it. That's what a connected system does — it makes sure nothing falls through the cracks."**

### 6. The Content & Automation Engine

Counter Cultures is invisible online right now. No blog. No email list. No consistent social presence. For a brand with this much story to tell — the artisans, the materials, the design heritage of San Miguel — that's a huge missed opportunity.

We're building a content system powered by n8n automation where one blog post automatically becomes social media content and an email newsletter. One piece of content becomes five. The team doesn't have to think about repurposing — the system handles it.

On the automation side: AI-powered lead qualification via WhatsApp chatbot (answers common questions, routes serious inquiries to the right rep), automated quote follow-ups (if a quote goes 48 hours without a response, the system nudges), post-sale review requests, and appointment reminders for showroom visits.

The team focuses on what they're good at — selling, specifying, building relationships. The system handles the repetitive follow-up work that currently falls through the cracks.

### 7. SEO & AEO — Becoming Visible

Explain this simply. SEO is how you show up on Google. AEO — Answer Engine Optimization — is the new frontier: how you show up when someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "where can I buy Kohler fixtures in Mexico?" Right now, Counter Cultures is invisible in both.

We've already built the keyword strategy — five clusters covering every way someone might search for what Counter Cultures sells, in English and Spanish. The custom-built site will have proper structured data baked in from day one (not bolted on after the fact), and sites with proper structured data get cited 3x more by AI search engines.

**"When an architect in Mexico City asks ChatGPT 'best Kohler distributor in Mexico,' Counter Cultures shows up. When a homeowner in SMA Googles 'artisan copper sink Mexico,' Counter Cultures shows up. That's what visibility means in 2026."**

### 8. The Build — Why Custom Matters

Take a moment to explain why we're building this custom instead of using off-the-shelf platforms. This is important for Roger to understand.

Most agencies would recommend Shopify or WordPress plus a bunch of plugins plus a separate CRM subscription plus a separate email tool. Roger ends up paying five different monthly fees to five different companies, and none of the tools talk to each other without expensive integrations that break.

What we're doing is different. We're building one connected system — the website, the dashboard, the automations — all custom, all integrated, all owned by Roger. The data lives in Google Drive and Google Sheets, which Roger's team already uses. The automations run through n8n, an open-source tool with no per-seat fees. The website is custom-built and hosted for a fraction of what a Shopify Plus subscription costs.

**Roger owns everything.** The code. The data. The automations. If he ever wants to bring in a different team to maintain it, he can — there's no proprietary platform locking him in. This is his system, built for his business.

### 9. Timeline — 7 Weeks

Phase 1 (Week 1): Map & Brand — finalize the brand direction, lock the color palette and typography, map the complete site architecture and dashboard requirements. A lot of this thinking is already done.

Phase 2 (Weeks 2–3): Build — the custom website and the dashboard go up simultaneously. By the end of week 3, Roger has a working site and a working dashboard he can see and click through.

Phase 3 (Weeks 4–5): Automate & Launch — wire up the automations (WhatsApp routing, lead assignment, content repurposing, follow-up sequences), migrate content, connect SEO, go live.

Phase 4 (Weeks 6–7): Optimize & Hand Off — train the team, refine based on real usage, optimize the dashboard views, deliver all documentation and training materials.

He'll have a working site and dashboard by week 3. By week 7, the entire connected system is live and the team knows how to use it.

### 10. After Launch — Two Options

Option A: Roger's team runs it independently. We hand off full documentation, training videos, and everything they need to manage the system themselves. The site, dashboard, and automations are designed to be maintained without a developer for day-to-day operations.

Option B: Untold Works stays on as a monthly partner — handling content, optimization, technical updates, and strategic support. Most clients start with B while they get comfortable, then transition to A when they're ready. No lock-in, no long-term contracts.

### 11. Questions Roger Might Have

Weave answers to these naturally into the conversation — don't present them as a Q&A list. Anticipate what Roger is thinking and address it before he has to ask:

- **"Why not just fix the Squarespace site?"** — Counter Cultures has outgrown Squarespace. It can't handle trade accounts, has no real automation, and there's no way to connect it to a dashboard or CRM. The limitations are structural, not cosmetic.
- **"Will my team be able to update the site?"** — Yes. Adding products, changing prices, publishing blog posts — the system is designed so your team handles this without calling a developer.
- **"What about our Google rankings?"** — Full URL redirects from every old Squarespace page. No broken links, no lost rankings. We handle this completely.
- **"How does WhatsApp work with multiple reps?"** — Shared WhatsApp Business inbox. Multiple team members see and respond. Automated templates for common responses. Leads get assigned to specific reps automatically.
- **"Do we have enough content for a blog?"** — We build the initial content — brand spotlights, artisan stories, material guides, design trend pieces. Plus the automation means one blog post becomes a social post, an email, and more. One piece becomes five.
- **"Is 7 weeks realistic?"** — Yes. Phase 1 (strategy and brand direction) is largely done through this work. Phase 2 is focused building. Phases 3–4 are automation, polish, and training. It's fast because we've already done the thinking.
- **"What does the monthly retainer look like?"** — Depends on what Roger wants. Some clients need weekly content and ongoing optimization. Others just need technical maintenance. We scope it together. No lock-in.
- **"What about the artisan suppliers?"** — They're the star. The Artisanal Collection gets its own dedicated landing page with maker profiles and process stories. This is the content no competitor can replicate — and it's some of the most powerful SEO and AEO content we'll create.
- **"What if we want to change something later? Are we locked into your team?"** — No. Roger owns the code, the data, the automations. Everything lives on his Google account and his hosting. Any developer can pick it up. That's the point of building custom — no vendor lock-in.

### 12. The Close — It's About the System, Not the Store

End with conviction and warmth. Bring it full circle.

**"Roger, the showroom is great. Nobody is questioning the showroom. But the showroom is one room. What we're building is the system that surrounds it — the system that captures every lead before they walk in, follows up after they leave, keeps Counter Cultures visible to people who haven't heard of it yet, and gives you real numbers on how the business is performing every single day.**

**The store is where people buy. The connected system is how you make sure they find you, choose you, and come back."**

Counter Cultures has the products. The showroom. The 20-year reputation. The artisan story that nobody in Mexico can copy. What's missing is the digital infrastructure — the connected system — that turns all of that into something that scales. Something Roger can see. Something the team can rely on. Something that works even when everyone is busy in the showroom.

**"No more lost leads. No more guessing. No more invisible. One connected system — custom-built for Counter Cultures."**
