A Walkthrough

How a single inquiry
becomes a delivered order —
in eleven quiet steps.

Counter Cultures sells imported plumbing and architectural fixtures — Kohler, Brizo, BLANCO, TOTO — to architects, designers and builders across Mexico. Inquiries arrive through six doors: a WhatsApp message from a phone Roger doesn't know, a Meta DM on Instagram or Facebook, a phone call, a cold email, a walk-in at the SMA showroom, or a website inquiry from countercultures.com.mx. A single deal threads through that first touch, a quote in two languages, a fiscal CFDI, a vendor in another country, customs paperwork, and a signed delivery slip in San Miguel. The portal is the one place all of that lives.

  • Six doors in, eleven stages out
  • From first touch to signed receipt
  • Across eight systems, one tab

Before any of this

A morning brief, tailored to each chair.

Every user opens the dashboard to one curated card at the top of their day. Pipeline pulse on the left. The handful of things only they can unblock in the middle. What advanced overnight on the right. What's stuck across the bottom. No twenty-tabs, no Slack scroll, no "where did we leave off."

The brief is per-role. Roger's owner brief is the first one shipping — pipeline value, deals only he can move, the customs front, deals past their stage SLA. As the dashboard's Finance Director and Sales profiles get built out, each one will get its own brief, tuned to its work. The Finance brief lives in the AR / SPEI / fiscal anomaly world. Each sales rep sees only their own book of leads, quotes, and deals. Same shape, different content.

The brief regenerates on demand and is cached for five minutes; a snapshot is appended to a Google Sheet on every refresh so yesterday's brief is always recoverable. Future delivery channels — email at 7:55 AM and a WhatsApp summary — read from that same source so the dashboard, the inbox, and the phone never disagree.

Stage One

Six doors. One file.

Counter Cultures meets customers in six places — a phone-only WhatsApp ping, a Meta DM on Instagram or Facebook, a phone call, a cold email, a walk-in at the SMA showroom, or a website form. The portal accepts each one on its own terms and lands them all in the same row at /dashboard/leads.

The Source column carries that origin forward through every later stage: it's how the portal knows to send the quote back on WhatsApp and email when the lead came in on WhatsApp, email-only when it came in by website or Meta DM, and how the walk-in flow knows to capture an email up front so the quote can be sent the next day.

Stage Two

Every inquiry becomes a deal.

Roger's rule: every inquiry gets quoted. Walk-in, WhatsApp, website — same rule. Not every one closes — but every one gets a quote. One click converts the lead to CC-2026-0142 on the Pipeline kanban, and everything for this customer — quote, PO, CFDI, delivery receipt, notes — lives under that single Deal record.

The deal carries the Source field forward from the lead so later stages know how to behave: WhatsApp leads get the quote sent on both WhatsApp and email, walk-ins get a printed copy plus an email follow-up, website leads get email-only. At the same instant the system provisions a Google Drive folder under the Shared Drive with five subfolders. From this moment on, every PDF the deal ever produces lands here automatically. Nobody has to remember to file anything.

Stage Three

Stage Four

Email is the artifact. Always.

Every quote ships by email — non-negotiable, even when it's also handed across the counter on paper. The email is the digital record for the client and the marketing footprint for Counter Cultures. Roger's correction to the obvious read: walk-ins rarely leave with a printed quote. They look, they leave, and the quote goes out the next day.

The Source field on the deal sets the dialog's default channel; Roger can always override.

  • · WhatsApp source → opens to WhatsApp + email both. WhatsApp carries a short note and a link; email carries the full PDF and payment link.
  • · Walk-in source → opens to email only. Email captured on the lead form is required, because the quote is almost always sent the next day, not handed over at the counter. Print is a one-click extra for the rare small POS in-stock purchase.
  • · Website · Email · Meta DM → email only, unless the customer left an explicit phone and asked for WhatsApp.
  • · Phone source → opens to email + WhatsApp, since Roger usually has the number from the call.

The PDF saves to Deals/CC-2026-0142/Quotes/, a 30-day signed share link is generated, and the deal moves to Quoted. The customer never logs in. They just open their phone.

Stage Five

¿Requiere CFDI? Asked early.

Whether the customer needs a stamped CFDI changes everything that follows — what gets collected, which factura is issued, when the fiscal moment happens. So the portal asks the question before she pays, not after.

The dashboard answers her with one of three buckets — two on-books and one off-books — depending on how she pays and what she says about CFDI:

  • · Sí — CFDI. Constancia collected, stamped at deposit clear, XML + PDF in Drive.
  • · No — Público. Factura general al público, on-books, no fiscal data collected.
  • · Cash bucket. For Netpay-non-fiscal, cash, or check on a small in-stock walk-in. Off-books, earmarked for the same-month cash expenses Counter Cultures pays in cash — partial showroom + bodega rent, petty cash, salaries for the bookkeeper and manager. Honest accounting for Roger's eyes only; the cash-bucket ledger at /dashboard/finance/cash-bucket is owner-gated.

The bucket is recorded on the payment and drives downstream behavior — Sí stamps the CFDI; Público issues the factura at deposit; Cash bucket skips the fiscal moment and lands on the cash-bucket ledger where the earmark answers "where did this go?" at month-end.

Stage Six

Eleven ways to pay.

Counter Cultures takes the deposit through whichever channel suits the customer — and the right channel depends on which door they came through. WhatsApp and website leads usually pay by Stripe link, wire, or Zelle. Walk-in leads usually swipe at the counter on Santander Fiscal or Netpay, or hand over cash or a check. The portal handles all eleven methods so the books always agree with the fiscal record either way.

Default is 70% deposit; some customers pay 100% upfront. In-person, the fiscal/non-fiscal toggle is decided in the moment — the same card swipe can run as Santander Fiscal or as Netpay depending on what the customer is asked at the counter. If the answer in stage five was CFDI: yes, the moment payment clears, the CFDI stamps and emails to the customer.

Stage Seven

A default. Not a constraint.

The portal checks the warehouse before generating anything. If the SKU is in stock, no PO — the line item flips to From Stock and waits for shipping. If it isn't, a Purchase Order generates against the brand's default vendor: Kohler ships through Ferguson, BLANCO through JCR, Sun Valley Bronze direct.

But the default isn't a constraint. Roger sometimes routes the same brand through a different vendor — JCR has stock, Ferguson is faster on US-East lots, the artisan channel handles customs cleaner. The PO line item exposes a vendor dropdown with live decision context: stock on hand, lead time, and an import-ease score per alternative. When Roger overrides, the dropdown asks why, and the answer is saved on the PO so "why did this Kohler PO go through JCR?" has an answer three months from now.

Stage Eight

Paying the vendor, clearing the goods.

"Net 30" doesn't capture how vendors actually bill. Roger's vendors run on five distinct patterns, and the AP queue dispatches off each one — the right payment lands on the right day, out of the right account, and the PO send is gated when payment has to clear first.

  • · on-ship · net N. Ferguson. Vendor invoices once goods ship; due = ship date + terms.
  • · on-order · net N. Vendor invoices off the PO; due = PO date + terms.
  • · cash-upfront. JCR. Vendor won't start the order without payment cleared — send-PO is blocked until the advance posts.
  • · split 50 / 50. 50% at PO + 50% net N off ship date. Two queue rows, the first blocking.
  • · never-invoices. Sun Valley Bronze direct. No confirmation, no invoice — pay-against-PO with manual AP entry, surfaced in the queue with a clear badge.

Vendors also split on whether they confirm — Ferguson sends an email confirmation with estimated lead times before the invoice; the artisan channel never does. The Vendors sheet at /dashboard/vendors is the source of truth for both billing trigger and confirmation pattern.

Then the import side: commercial invoice, packing list, pedimento, broker handoff. The customs tracker runs through twelve stages — origin pickup, freight, port, SAT release, trucking — and every document lands in Pedimentos & Importación/ as it's produced. Roger sees one row that says where the goods actually are.

Stage Eight · Continued

Importación, end to end.

Crossing goods from a US consolidator into Mexico isn't one event — it's a sixteen-state lifecycle with three external counterparties (broker, crossing agent, TGR warehouse), eleven distinct documents, two SPEI payments, and an expediente that has to be physically signed at the end. The portal absorbs all of it under /dashboard/customs so Roger sees one row that always says exactly where the goods are and what's owed next.

Roger's goods are consolidated at TGR Logistic Inc. in Laredo, Texas. Antonina sends the batch of vendor invoices to broker Jeanefer Contreras; she runs them through Mexican customs against the cálculo (tax estimate); cleared goods cross at Lic. Carlos Enrique Garza's pass; a domestic carrier brings them to the SMA showroom. Every state transition writes back to the deal's timeline so customer-facing stages know when to advance.

The cálculo, line by line.

Every crossing carries three stacks of charges: customs taxes (IGI, IVA, DTA), broker fees on the Mexican side (honorarios, prevalidación, validación, sellos fiscales), and warehouse handling on the US side (revisión / clasificación, carga y descarga, etiquetas y manuales). The portal renders the broker's cálculo PDF as a structured table so Roger sees the real cost of each crossing without re-keying it into a spreadsheet.

Two SPEI payments leave Counter Cultures' Santander account: one for the taxes + broker fees (PED — to TGR Logistics / Gestores Aduanales del Noreste, BBVA Mexico) and one for the truck-crossing fee (CRUZ — to Lic. Carlos Enrique Garza Roque, Banamex). Both confirmations land back on the trafico row.

Stage Nine

Three ways it gets to her.

The fulfillment switch from stage three sets the path. Local SMA delivery goes to Miguel's calendar; date and time are set against the customer's preference and his availability, by phone. Pickup happens at the warehouse.

Every release ends with a signature. Miguel captures it on the customer's doorstep; the warehouse captures it at the counter. The signed receipt drops into Delivery Receipts/ the moment it's saved. No goods leave without it.
